What changes in ISO 9001:2026

ISO 9001 is being revised for the first time since 2015. Publication is expected in September 2026. Here is what is changing, what it means for a small shop, and what is worth doing now.

Status

Where the revision actually stands

The Final Draft International Standard went out for ballot in April 2026, and publication is expected in September 2026. At FDIS stage the content is essentially settled — remaining changes are editorial.

The people running the revision have described it as evolution, not revolution. The structure stays the same. Existing requirements are clarified rather than replaced. If you already run a system that works, you are not starting over.

One caveat, stated plainly: until the standard publishes, everything below is based on the draft and on published committee information. Exact wording can shift. Nothing here should be treated as the final text, and this page will be updated when the standard is released.

The Changes

Five themes worth knowing about

Risks and opportunities get separated. The 2015 version treated them together. The revision splits them into their own subclauses, with opportunities handled as a discipline of their own rather than a footnote to risk. If your current system logs risks and never mentions opportunities, that is the gap.
Quality culture and ethical behavior become explicit. A new awareness requirement covers the organization's quality culture and ethical conduct — not just whether people know the procedure, but whether they understand why it exists and are willing to speak up.
Strategic direction is emphasized. The quality policy is expected to connect to where the business is actually going, rather than being a framed statement nobody has read since the last audit.
Climate change enters organizational context. Climate considerations are folded into how you evaluate the context your business operates in. For most small shops this is a documentation matter, not an operational one — but it does need to be addressed rather than ignored.
Annex A is substantially revised. The guidance on how to interpret the requirements has been rewritten. This one matters more than it sounds — Annex A is what auditors and consultants lean on when a requirement is ambiguous.

The Harmonized Structure — the common clause layout shared across ISO management system standards — is retained. Clause numbering you already know largely stays put.

If You Are Already Certified

You will have a transition window. Confirm it with your registrar.

Revisions of this kind normally come with a defined transition period, during which certificates to the old version stay valid and organizations move to the new one at a surveillance or recertification audit.

The length of that window is set by the accreditation bodies, not by us. Call your registrar and ask two questions: what is my transition deadline, and which of my scheduled audits will be the transition audit? Both answers should be free, and both change your planning.

If You Are Not Certified Yet

This is better timing than it looks.

If a customer has just started asking and you are building a system from scratch, you are in a better position than a shop that certified in 2018 and now has to change. You get to build against the current thinking once, instead of building twice.

The practical move is to build the system on the parts that are not changing — documented process, records, nonconformance and corrective action, internal audit, management review — and treat the new themes as additions rather than a different foundation.

What To Do Now

Three things, none of which cost money

1

Call your registrar

If you are certified: get your transition deadline and your transition audit date in writing. Put both on a calendar.

2

Look at opportunities

Of the five themes, this is the one most existing systems handle worst. If your risk log has no opportunity side, start one now — it costs nothing and it is the most likely finding.

3

Wait on the rest

Do not buy a transition package before the standard publishes. Anyone selling you a definitive gap analysis against an unpublished document is selling you a guess.
