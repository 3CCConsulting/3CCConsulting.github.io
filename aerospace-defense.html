Aerospace & Defense Shops & Suppliers

Your prime is citing AS9100. Most of what they'll find isn't the ISO half.

AS9100D contains all of ISO 9001 and adds roughly a hundred aviation, space, and defense requirements on top of it. Small suppliers rarely fail the ISO half. They fail on the additions — the ones nobody warned them about until the audit.

Where It Actually Goes Wrong

Six places a small supplier gets written up.

Open any one. These are the additions on top of ISO 9001, roughly in the order they cost small shops findings.

Configuration management

Knowing which revision is authorized, which one is on the floor, and which one shipped — and being able to prove all three were the same.

This is the one that turns a good shop into a nonconformance. The work gets done right; the record of which drawing it was done to doesn't survive the year.

Counterfeit part prevention

A documented process for how you verify material is what the certificate says it is, and what happens when it isn't.

Most shops have a purchasing habit — a supplier they trust, a mill cert they file. The standard wants a process, with a decision rule and a record. Those are different things, and only one of them survives an audit.

Special process control

Welding, heat treat, NDT, finishing — qualified, controlled, and evidenced. Operator qualification current, parameters recorded, the process itself approved.

This one I don't have to read up on. I welded for twenty years to ONET, WABO and NADCAP qualification and I hold an AWS B5.2 weld inspector certificate, so I know what a special process control has to survive on a real floor. I've been the one getting audited against it.

First article inspection

AS9102 forms, filled out correctly, the first time — with every characteristic accounted for and the ballooned print to match.

Rejected FAIs are rarely about bad parts. They're about paperwork that doesn't tie back to the drawing cleanly, and about nobody owning the form.

Product safety and risk

Product safety is its own requirement, not something folded into general quality. Safety-critical items get identified and handled differently, and people need a route to raise a concern.

Expect an auditor to ask how somebody on your floor flags a problem without putting their name on it. If the honest answer is "they'd tell me," that's a finding waiting to happen.

Supply chain flowdown

Your customer's requirements have to reach your suppliers in writing — and you have to be able to show they did.

Your subs' work becomes your work in the prime's eyes. If a requirement stopped at your purchasing desk instead of reaching the heat treater, the nonconformance is yours.

AS9100 is being renamed and revised.

It becomes IA9100, aligned to the ISO 9001 revision underneath it, and the IAQG has signalled a shift to frequent smaller updates rather than one revision a decade. That changes how a system should be built, not just what it has to say.
